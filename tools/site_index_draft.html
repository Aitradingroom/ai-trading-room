Strategy research log

IBS mean reversion (single index + index basket)

Weak

MT5 EA + Pine

Buy a stock index after a weak close: IBS = (close − low) / (high − low) below a threshold and the close below a pullback band (highest high of 10 days − 2.5 × average range of 25 days). Exit when the close is above the previous day's high. The basket version runs the same rule on several indices with a cap on total exposure.

Finding: The edge is real but small on CFD indices once spread and financing are paid. The first basket version had a −40% worst month because the indices fall together; a gross-exposure cap fixed the blow-up but also most of the return.

Index system: turn-of-the-month + IBS

Weak

MT5 EA

Two documented stock-index effects in one basket: (A) long from the last trading day of the month into the first days of the new month (McConnell & Xu 2008), (B) the IBS pullback rule above. Positions are sized by volatility targeting.

Finding: Combining two weak edges smoothed the curve but did not make it worth trading after costs.

G10 currency basket: carry, momentum, value

Failed

MT5 EA

Every month rank the G10 currencies against USD on carry (policy rate), momentum and value, go long the best and short the worst.

Finding: The result was dominated by broker swap. The MT5 tester applies today's swap rates to all of history, so long backtests of carry strategies on CFDs are unreliable. A bad USDCHF tick on 15 Jan 2015 (SNB day) also has to be excluded.

Lessons so far

  1. Costs decide everything. Spread, commission and overnight swap turned several published edges into losers on retail CFDs.
  2. The MT5 tester applies today's swap to all of history, so long-hold and carry backtests on CFDs need care.
  3. In-sample is not enough. Wherever possible a result is re-checked on a later, unseen period.
  4. A smooth balance is not safety. Grid systems without a stop show a rising balance while equity sinks.
